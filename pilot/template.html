Fly Pilot

Real: the 264 neurons and ~4,000 connections drawn here, with their transmitters, are the fly's actual flight-stabilisation pathway from the MaleCNS v1.0 connectome (Janelia, Google Research and collaborators, CC BY 4.0, via neuPrint). Rotation-detecting cells in the optic lobes (HS, VS, H1, H2) feed descending neurons such as DNa02, which drive interneurons and wing-steering motor neurons in the nerve cord.

Simulated: each neuron is a single firing rate passed along those connections, excitatory or inhibitory by transmitter. The plane's rotation is fed to the visual neurons as the motion a fly would see.

Learned: a fly has no ailerons. A 3 × 15 map from its left/right wing motor neurons to the plane's controls starts random and is learned by trial and error: each pair of attempts tries a small change both ways through the same gusts and keeps the better one. The brain's wiring itself never changes.

No fly is involved and this isn't a fly thinking. It's a real wiring diagram running a cartoon of neural activity.
