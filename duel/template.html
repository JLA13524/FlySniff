How it learned

Each dot on the axis is a saved brain you can pick above. Early on they drift about waving at nothing; then they learn to close in, swing on purpose and block. The steps in the curve are rule changes: around 0.4M steps both flies learned to hover at the ceiling trading near-misses to farm dodge points without ever hitting, so dodges became zero-sum (the swinger loses what the dodger gains); later they gave up flying for ground fights, so walking was made slow and flying fast, as for a real fly.

What's going on

Same idea as the viral clip: virtual flies trained with PPO, a standard trial-and-error learning algorithm, by fighting copies of themselves. No connectome is involved; each fly's "brain" above is a small neural network (24 senses, two layers of 64, 6 actions).

They can fly. Each fly controls height, blade swing and tilt, and a sideways dash with a one-second cooldown. A hit is +1 and being hit is −1; getting out of the way of a swing that would have landed earns a small bonus, so dodging is learned too.

Simpler than the clip: simplified fly bodies and physics, trained right here in about half an hour of CPU.
